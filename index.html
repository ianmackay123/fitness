Built for a mat, a pair of adjustable dumbbells, and nothing else

Two daysa week.

Before you start each session

Five minutes: 20 arm circles each way, 10 slow bodyweight squats, 10 hip hinges with no weight, 10 press-ups against a wall. You want to feel warm, not tired.

How the weight decisions get made

You do not have to think about this during a session. Log the weight and reps for each set and the plan works out the rest. The rule underneath it:

  1. Leave one or two reps in the tank. Stop a set when you could have done one or two more with good form, not when you physically cannot move.
  2. Hit the top of the rep range on every set, three sessions in a row at the same weight, and it goes up one step. Each exercise comes round once a week, so that is roughly one step every three weeks.
  3. Fall below the bottom of the range on any set, two sessions in a row, and it drops one step. One bad day changes nothing.
  4. Anything in between, same weight. Keep chasing the top of the range on every set.
  5. Give it eight weeks. Strength moves in a few sessions, visible size takes longer.

The part the training will not do for you

There is no exercise that burns fat off one specific area. Three things matter, in this order: eat a few hundred calories less than you burn, get 1.6 to 2.2g of protein per kilo of bodyweight a day, and walk 8,000 to 10,000 steps daily. The lifting is what keeps the weight you lose from being muscle. Track the steps the way you track the lifts, or that goal will quietly not happen.

If something hurts in a joint rather than burns in a muscle, stop that movement and check the cues again. General guidance only, not medical advice.
